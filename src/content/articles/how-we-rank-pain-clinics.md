Every health directory claims to be independent. The claim is only worth
something if you can check it, so this article is the whole thing in the open:
the five signals we score, what each one is worth, where the numbers come from,
and the exact point at which money enters the picture — because it does, and
pretending otherwise would be the least trustworthy sentence on the page.

## The five signals, and what each is worth

| Signal | Weight |
|---|---|
| Interventional pain capability | 25% |
| Verified patient rating | 25% |
| Verified review volume | 20% |
| Years in practice | 15% |
| Contact transparency | 15% |

Those percentages are not a description of the ranking written separately from
it. The [methodology page](/how-we-rank/) renders its numbers from the same
constant the scoring function uses, so the published weights cannot quietly
drift away from the ones actually running.

**Interventional pain capability (25%).** Whether the practice carries the
interventional pain medicine taxonomy — code 208VP0014X — in the federal NPI
registry. That is the practice's own declaration that image-guided procedures
are core to its scope rather than an occasional add-on. It is public, and you
can look up any clinic's record yourself.

**Verified patient rating (25%)** and **review volume (20%).** The aggregate
rating from public sources, plus how many reviews stand behind it. Volume is
deliberately log-scaled and capped, which is a technical way of saying two
things: one glowing review cannot carry a clinic, and a good practice with
forty reviews is not buried under a chain with three thousand.

**Years in practice (15%).** Time since the practice's national provider record
was established, capped at twenty years — long enough to separate a brand-new
storefront from an established practice, capped so a forty-year-old clinic does
not get to coast past a solid fifteen-year-old one.

**Contact transparency (15%).** Whether the clinic publishes a working website
and a phone number. It sounds trivial. In a field where the practices worth
avoiding are frequently the hardest ones to identify or contact, it is not.

## Why these five

Every one of them is verifiable by someone who is not us. That was the filter.

We would love to score bedside manner, whether the physician listened, whether
the injection helped. We cannot measure any of that honestly at national scale,
so we do not pretend to. What is conspicuously missing from the list is just as
deliberate: nothing we would have to take a clinic's word for, and nothing we
would have to invent.

Two additions are on the list and not yet in the score: insurance breadth and
the range of procedures a practice actually offers. When they land, we will say
so here. Methodology changes get published, not slipped in.

## A low score is usually a data gap, not a verdict

This is the part worth understanding before you use the numbers.

Scores land in four bands: **Strongly qualified** at 70 and above, **Well
qualified** at 50, **Qualified** at 30, and below that a **Basic listing** —
where we deliberately hide the number entirely.

We hide it because at the bottom of the scale the score stops describing the
clinic and starts describing us. A small, excellent, decades-old practice that
has never collected an online review and runs a one-page website will score
badly on three of our five signals. That is a gap in what we can verify, not a
judgment about the care. Showing "22 / 100" next to that practice would be a
lie told with a real number.

So treat a high score as a reason to shortlist and a low one as a reason to ask
more questions — not as a grade.

## Where money actually enters

Here is the honest accounting.

**Listing is free, and not optional.** Every pain practice in the federal data
appears here whether or not it has ever heard of us. There is no version of this
directory where a clinic is missing because it did not pay.

**Claiming is free.** A practice can verify it represents itself, correct its
own address, hours, and phone, and earn a Verified badge — at no cost.

**Enhanced ($29/month)** buys profile *content*: photos, a custom description,
services and accepted insurance, provider bios, a booking link. It makes a
listing more complete. It does not move it.

**Featured ($299/month)** buys a labeled sponsored slot — one per area,
displayed above the ranked list in its own box, marked "Sponsored placement"
with a link back to the methodology. It is advertising, and it looks like
advertising.

The structural guarantee sits underneath all of that. The function that scores a
clinic receives five inputs: taxonomy code, rating, review count, provider-record
date, and whether a website and phone exist. The listing tier is not one of
them — it is not passed in, so it cannot be weighed. A paying clinic and a free
clinic with identical public records receive identical scores, and the ranked
list below the sponsored box is ordered exactly as it would be if nobody had
paid us anything.

One more disclosure, since it is the conflict directories usually bury: the
organization behind this site owns no pain clinics. If that ever changes, you
will read it in a site-wide notice and on every affiliated listing, and the
criteria above will not change to flatter them. That commitment lives on our
[ownership disclosure](/ownership-disclosure/) page.

## Using the ranking well

The score narrows a list. It does not pick a clinic. Once you have three or four
candidates in [the directory](/pain-clinics/), the work that actually matters is
the work only you can do: figuring out [which kind of pain clinic fits your
situation](/news/how-to-choose-a-pain-clinic/), and running the phone calls and
website checks in our guide to [spotting a practice worth
avoiding](/news/spot-a-pill-mill-red-flags/).

We would rather publish a methodology you can argue with than a number you have
to trust.
