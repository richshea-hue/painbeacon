If you have ever searched "best pain clinic near me," you have seen a lot of
lists. Very few of them tell you how the order was decided. That silence is
usually the answer: on many directories, position is inventory, and the clinic
at the top is the clinic that bought the top.

We think you are entitled to know what you are looking at. So here is the whole
mechanism — the signals, their weights, the things we refuse to score, and the
honest limits of the whole exercise.

## What actually moves a clinic up

Every practice in the directory gets a score out of 100, built from five
signals. Nothing else touches it. The current weighting, which is published
live on [our methodology page](/how-we-rank/) and generated from the same code
that does the ranking:

- **Interventional pain capability — 25%.** Whether the practice carries the
  interventional pain medicine credential in the federal provider registry
  (taxonomy code 208VP0014X). In plain terms: this is a practice equipped to do
  image-guided procedures, not only to manage medication.
- **Verified patient rating — 25%.** The aggregate rating from public sources,
  scaled onto our 0–25 range.
- **Verified review volume — 20%.** How many reviews that rating rests on.
  Volume is log-scaled and capped, so a practice with 200 reviews and one with
  2,000 land close together — and a single glowing five-star review cannot
  outrun a long track record.
- **Years in practice — 15%.** Time since the practice's national provider
  record was established, capped at twenty years so a forty-year-old practice
  and a twenty-five-year-old one are not artificially separated.
- **Contact transparency — 15%.** Whether the clinic publishes a working
  website and a working phone number.

Ties break on review count, then alphabetically. That is the entire algorithm.
There is no editor's thumb and no "partner boost."

## What we deliberately refuse to score

The list above is short, and the omissions are the point. We do not score how
much a practice spends on marketing, how new its building is, or how polished
its website looks. Not because those things are irrelevant to your experience —
a calm, comfortable office genuinely matters — but because none of them can be
checked from the outside, and anything that cannot be checked can be bought.

We also do not pretend to measure things we cannot yet see in the data.
Insurance breadth and the range of procedures a clinic offers are the two most
useful signals we are missing. Rather than approximate them with something
flimsy, we have left them out and said so — and when they go in, we will publish
the change instead of quietly reordering the site overnight.

## What the score is not

A score is a measure of qualification, not a verdict on care. It says a
practice is credentialed, established, contactable, and well reviewed. It
cannot tell you whether a particular physician will listen to you, or whether
their approach fits your particular back.

That distinction shows up in how we display low scores. Clinics fall into four
bands — Strongly qualified, Well qualified, Qualified, and Basic listing — and
for that lowest band we hide the number entirely. A low score usually means we
hold thin verified data on a practice, not that the practice is bad. Printing
"22 out of 100" beside a small, perfectly good clinic that simply has few
online reviews would be a failing grade we have no standing to give.

## So what does a clinic get for money?

Practices can pay us, and you should know exactly what that buys, because
vagueness here is how the whole category lost its credibility.

Every practice in the country is listed for free, ranked the same way, whether
or not it has ever heard of us. A practice can claim its listing at no cost and
correct its address, hours, phone, and website. Paid tiers add *content*: photos,
a fuller description, services and conditions treated, accepted insurance,
provider bios. A separate Featured option buys a placement on local pages that
is labeled as advertising and sits outside the ranked list.

None of it touches the score. Paying us cannot move a clinic up one position,
and the guard is structural rather than a promise — the ranking code reads only
the five signals above, and none of them are editable by the practice. The
signals that *are* practice-editable, like the website and phone number that
feed contact transparency, are worth points precisely because we can verify
them ourselves.

If our incentives ever change, you will read it here first. We publish an
[ownership disclosure](/ownership-disclosure/) for the same reason: a directory
that ranks clinics while quietly owning some of them has a problem worth naming
out loud.

## How to use the ranking (and when to ignore it)

Treat the order as a shortlist generator, not a verdict. The ranking is good at
what a spreadsheet is good at: filtering thousands of practices down to the
credentialed, established, well-reviewed handful near you. It is bad at
everything that happens after that — which is most of what matters.

So use it to build a list of three or four, then do the human part: check the
physicians' credentials yourself, call the front desk, and ask the questions in
our [guide to choosing a pain clinic](/news/how-to-choose-a-pain-clinic/). A
clinic ranked fourth that returns your call and requests your imaging is a
better choice than one ranked first that does neither.

You can see every clinic we hold, with its signals shown openly, in
[the directory](/pain-clinics/). If a listing about your own practice is wrong,
tell us and we will fix it — for free, and without it changing your rank one
way or the other.
