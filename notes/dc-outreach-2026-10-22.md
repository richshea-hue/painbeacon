# DC-area outreach — advertisers (Mark) and clinics (internal)
*2026-10-22. Numbers pulled from Supabase the same day; re-run the queries before quoting them.*

Two campaigns that share a market and nothing else. Mark sells **sponsorships** to
businesses that are not clinics. We send **awareness mail** to the clinics themselves,
who are never charged. Keeping those separate is not bookkeeping — a clinic that thinks
the directory is selling it something stops answering, and the whole claim flow depends
on them answering.

# Part 1 — Mark: selling DMV sponsorships

## What he is actually selling

| Tier | Monthly | 3 months prepaid | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **State group** | **$750/mo** | **$1,800** ($600/mo) | A metro's states sold together — **VA + MD + DC is the named example on /advertise/** |
| Single state | $400/mo | $1,000 ($333/mo) | One state alone |
| National | $3,000/mo | $7,200 ($2,400/mo) | Every state, one sponsor per category nationwide |

The DMV group is the product for this market. Most buyers here serve all three
jurisdictions, and $750 covers all three where two singles would cost $800.

**What the buyer gets that nobody else sells them:**

1. **Category exclusivity by state.** One law firm, one device maker, one therapy
   network per state. Their competitor cannot buy in beside them. This is the whole
   pitch — it is scarcity, not reach, and it is true today while the inventory is empty.
2. **Measurement without surveillance.** No tracking scripts, no cookies. Views and
   clicks counted at the edge, clicks forwarded with UTM tags so their own analytics
   show PainBeacon as a source. Monthly report by page.
3. **Placement next to a decision.** The card renders below the ranked clinic list on
   area pages, after the profile on clinic pages — where someone has just finished
   comparing pain clinics. Never inside a ranked list, never on the map, never on
   the homepage.

## Where the low-hanging fruit actually is

Ranked by how fast they close, not by how much they can pay. Every one of these is a
flat monthly advertising fee — see the compliance line below for why that matters.

**Tier 1 — call these first**

1. **Physical therapy and rehab networks.** Multi-site, already buying local digital,
   decision-maker is an owner or a marketing director rather than a committee, and
   their customer is literally the person reading a pain-clinic page. *Caution:* use
   pure-play PT groups. The therapy arm of an orthopedic group is often already in our
   directory as a clinic, and a clinic cannot be a sponsor.
2. **Orthotics, bracing and durable medical equipment suppliers.** Same reader, high
   margin, and they are used to paying for placement.
3. **Compounding pharmacies.** Topical pain compounds are their growth product and
   they cannot advertise them on most platforms.

**Tier 2 — worth the call, longer cycle**

4. **Imaging centers** (standing/open MRI especially).
5. **Mattress, recliner and ergonomic seating retailers.** Zero healthcare regulatory
   surface, obvious fit, nobody else is selling them this.
6. **Massage, acupuncture and chiropractic groups** — only where they are not
   directory-listed clinics.
7. **Medical-legal funding companies.**
8. **Telehealth and regenerative-medicine programs.**

**The DC-specific angle nobody else can use**

This region has the largest federal workforce in the country, and federal employees
injured at work go through OWCP/FECA, not state workers' comp. Firms, PT practices and
DME suppliers that specialize in **federal worker injury claims** are a genuinely local
niche with money and almost no good advertising inventory. Same for the military and
veteran community around Bethesda, Belvoir and Quantico. Worth one prospecting pass of
its own — but the law-firm half of it is frozen until Paul decides.

## The approach

**Opener (phone, 20 seconds).**

> "I'm calling from PainBeacon — we're the independent directory of pain clinics, we
> list every one in Maryland, Virginia and DC. We sell one sponsorship per category per
> state, and nobody has taken [physical therapy] here yet. Are you the person who'd
> decide something like that?"

That sentence does the whole job: who we are, what is scarce, and whether this is the
right human. If it is not, ask for the name of who is and get off the phone.

**The email, if they ask for one.**

> Subject: One physical therapy sponsor per state — Maryland, Virginia, DC
>
> [Name],
>
> PainBeacon lists every pain medicine clinic in the country from federal records and
> ranks them on published criteria that are not for sale. People read those pages while
> they are deciding where to go for their back or their neck.
>
> We sell one sponsorship per category per state. No physical therapy group has taken
> Maryland, Virginia or DC. If you take it, your competitors can't buy in beside you
> for as long as you hold it.
>
> $750 a month for all three, or $1,800 for a quarter. The card is labeled
> Advertisement, it never touches our rankings, and we run no tracking scripts — but
> every view and click is counted and forwarded to you with UTM tags, so your own
> analytics see us as a source. You get a report by page every month.
>
> Worth fifteen minutes?
>
> Mark Mooney · PainBeacon

**Handling "how much traffic do you have?"** — this is the question that decides whether
Mark keeps his credibility, and the honest answer is the better sales answer. The site
is young and the traffic is small. He should say so, and sell the position instead:

> "Small and growing — I'll show you the real numbers, and I'll show you the report
> format so you can see we don't inflate them. What you're buying at this price is the
> category before it's expensive. When the traffic is big, this slot won't be $750 and
> it probably won't be available."

Never quote a number he cannot produce in a report. The one time this site nearly sent
an advertiser an inflated figure — 257 "clicks" that were really 5 — is the reason the
whole counting pipeline got rebuilt. Honest smallness is a feature here; it is the same
promise as "rankings are never for sale," and it is the only durable thing we sell.

**Founding-sponsor lever:** single-state founding sponsors get a share of the fee
reinvested in local search ads pointed at the pages they sponsor. That is on
`/advertise/` and it is a real closer for a hesitant first buyer.

## The guardrails — read before the first call

- **Flat fee only, never per referral or per patient.** EKRA (18 U.S.C. § 220) makes
  per-referral payment a crime for recovery homes, treatment facilities and labs, and
  it applies to private insurance, not just federal programs. The Anti-Kickback Statute
  covers the rest. Our product is a flat monthly advertising fee and must stay one —
  no "per lead," no revenue share, no bonus for volume, in any direction.
- **Decline the categories that bring that risk with them**: no sober-living or
  addiction treatment facilities, no clinical labs, no anything that wants to pay for
  patients rather than for placement. If a prospect starts negotiating toward per-lead,
  that is the end of the conversation, not the start of a creative one.
- **We never broker or steer referrals**, to a sponsor or anyone else.
- **Attorney and health advertising must comply with the rules of each state it appears
  in**, and the card carries a disclosure line where required ("Attorney advertising").
  We decline creative making a medical claim. This is already on `/advertise/`.
- **No tracking scripts, ever.** A sponsor asking to drop a pixel is a no — the privacy
  page is a promise the whole site rests on.

# Part 2 — Clinics: the DMV awareness batch

## What is actually there

Across the 22 DC-metro zones (DC, the Maryland suburbs, Northern Virginia):

| | count |
|---|---|
| Clinic records in the DMV zones | **357** |
| With a website (emailable after discovery) | 132 |
| With a phone (all of them) | 357 |
| **De-duplicated independent practices, 20–600 reviews** | **92** |
| …of those, with a website | **69** |

That last row is the real batch. Three things shrink 357 to 92, and each one matters:

1. **Duplicate NPIs.** Advanced Pain Management Specialists appears three times in one
   Waldorf address; Advanced Spine and Pain Centers three times in Stafford. Mailing a
   practice three times is the fastest way to look like a spammer. **De-duplicate on
   (normalized name, city) before sending** — `build_targets.py` does not do this today.
2. **Hospital systems.** MedStar, Kaiser, Inova and the big orthopedic groups top the
   review counts and will never answer marketing email. Filter them out.
3. **The dead tail.** Records with no reviews are often stale NPI entries; they are not
   worth a stamp until we have a reason to think someone is there.

Multi-site groups share one phone (Advanced Pain Management Specialists: 855-527-7246
across Germantown, Bethesda, Bowie, Largo, Waldorf). Work them as **one account**, not
five listings — and if they claim, five pages improve at once.

## Why this is also the backlink campaign

Search Console reports exactly two external referring domains for painbeacon.com, both
SEO-spam link sellers, both disavowed. The site ranks badly because nothing links to it.

The `badge-backlink` variant asks a clinic to claim its free listing and offers an
embeddable "Verified" badge for the clinic's own website. **Every clinic that takes the
badge is a real, topically relevant backlink from a local medical practice** — which is
the single hardest thing for this site to buy and the thing it most needs. Ten claims in
the DMV would be worth more than the sponsorship revenue from the same month.

So: run `badge-backlink`, not `fix-info`. Same list, better second-order payoff.

## Running it

From the repo on the Windows box — these need `.env` and network, so they will not run
in a cloud session:

```
python scripts/outreach/build_targets.py --list-markets --top 40
```

```
python scripts/outreach/build_targets.py --markets washington-area-dc,rockville-md,bowie-md,silver-spring-md,bethesda-md,clinton-md,greenbelt-md,laurel-md,oxon-hill-md,germantown-md,hyattsville-md,lanham-md,olney-md,sterling-va,fairfax-va,woodbridge-area-va,alexandria-area-va,arlington-area-va,falls-church-va --out scripts/outreach/out/dmv.csv
```

```
python scripts/outreach/discover_emails.py --in scripts/outreach/out/dmv.csv --out scripts/outreach/out/dmv_emails.csv
```

```
python scripts/outreach/make_drafts.py --in scripts/outreach/out/dmv_emails.csv --variant badge-backlink --from-name "Rich" --postal "<real mailing address>" --max 25
```

Open the drafts and read them before anything is sent — nothing auto-sends, by design.
Send 25, wait a week, read the reply rate before sending the rest. `out/` is gitignored
because it holds harvested emails; keep it local.

**Before the send**, de-duplicate and drop the systems. Until `build_targets.py` learns
to do it, a pass by eye over 132 rows takes ten minutes and is worth it.
