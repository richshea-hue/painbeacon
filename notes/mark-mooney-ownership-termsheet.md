# A Path to Ownership — non-binding term sheet for Mark Mooney, 2026-09-23

Source text for the PDF built by `scripts/build-mooney-termsheet.py`. Companion
to `notes/mark-mooney-sales-agreement.md`, which is the binding document.

## Why this is a term sheet and not a grant

There is no PainBeacon entity. Nothing can be granted, because there are no
units and no issuer. That constraint is not a weakness of the document — it is
the reason the document is honest. A signed "ownership agreement" naming a
company that does not exist would be worth less than this page, because it
would imply Mark has something he does not.

The Sales Agreement's Section 9 says it grants no ownership. This page does not
change that, and says so in its first paragraph.

## Design decisions and why

**Earned against revenue, not vested over time.** Four tranches against
cumulative gross revenue on sales Mark originates: 2% at $50K, 2% at $150K, 3%
at $300K, 3% at $500K, capped at 10%. Time vesting pays for showing up. What
Rich needs is sales, so the only thing that moves the number is sales.

**$500,000 is roughly 167 book-series sales**, or a mix with advertising. At
that volume the business is real, and 10% of its profit is a fair price for the
person who produced the top line — on top of commissions he already took.

**Revenue counts from the Sales Agreement's effective date, entity or no
entity.** Mark starts selling before the LLC exists. Losing credit for that
would be the first thing he resents.

**Section 4 is the backstop for the same problem**: if a tranche is earned and
no entity exists 24 months later, Rich owes him a contractual profit share with
the same economics. Without it, "I'll form it someday" is an unbounded promise.

**Non-voting profits interests, not capital interests.** Two reasons:

- *Control.* Rich keeps every decision. Mark is a first salesperson with
  upside, not a co-founder.
- *Tax.* A profits interest carrying a threshold equal to the entity's value at
  grant is generally not taxable to the recipient at grant under Rev. Proc.
  93-27, because it shares only in future appreciation. A capital interest
  would be compensation income to Mark on day one — a tax bill for something he
  cannot sell. This is the single most valuable technical choice on the page,
  and it argues for an LLC taxed as a partnership rather than an S-corp.

**Set the threshold value the day the entity forms.** It is the value Mark's
units sit above. Forming later, once revenue exists, raises it and makes the
same 10% worth less to him. Worth telling him, which the PDF does.

**Distributions, not exit-only equity.** There may never be a sale. Equity that
only pays on an exit is a lottery ticket; equity that pays distributions is
compensation. Rich sets the working reserve, since he runs it.

**Section 6 ties the equity to the invariant.** Breaching the rankings-not-for-
sale or no-referral-compensation sections forfeits unearned tranches and lets
PainBeacon buy back earned units at the lower of the formula or $1.00. The
directory's independence is the asset; a share of it cannot be worth more than
it. This is the strongest available lever on the one behavior that could end
the business, and it costs nothing to include.

**Drag-along is not optional.** A 10% holder who can block a sale is a 10%
holder who controls it. Tag-along is the fair counterweight and costs little.

**No anti-dilution**, stated plainly rather than omitted.

## Numbers Rich should have in his head

On one $3,000 book sale after recoupment: PainBeacon retains $728.17 after
Mark's 25% commission. 10% of that is **$72.82** — about 2.4% of what the clinic
paid. On a sale Mark originates he earns about $315 all in. On a sale he had
nothing to do with, he earns the $72.82 and nothing else.

That contrast is the argument for the whole structure, and it is the line to
use in the conversation: the commission pays for the sale, the units pay for
the business.

## What Mark needs to hear out loud

- **10% is 10% of PainBeacon**, which receives one third of book revenue. It is
  not 10% of a book sale. Paul's two-thirds is contractual and he holds no
  interest in PainBeacon.
- **Becoming a member changes his tax paperwork.** A partner in an LLC is
  generally not a contractor for tax purposes; his commissions would most
  likely be guaranteed payments on a K-1 rather than a 1099-NEC, with
  self-employment tax on his distributive share. The cash does not change. He
  should ask his own accountant before the first tranche is earned.
